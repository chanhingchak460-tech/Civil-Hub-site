Lab 01

Bending and deflection of a beam

ObjectiveRelate applied load, bending moment, surface strain and deflection for a simply supported beam.

Method

  1. Set the supports at the required span and check the beam is level.
  2. Attach strain gauges at midspan and connect them to the readout.
  3. Apply the load in increments and record strain and dial-gauge readings.
  4. Unload and check that the readings return close to zero.

Precautions

  • Do not overload the beam beyond its elastic range.
  • Tap the dial gauge lightly to check it is free.
  • Allow the beam to settle before reading the deflection.
  • Keep the load hanger from swinging.

Lab 02

Torsion of a circular shaft

ObjectiveMeasure the angle of twist of a shaft under torque and estimate the shear modulus.

Method

  1. Clamp the shaft and fit the torque arm and protractor.
  2. Apply torque in equal increments.
  3. Read the angle of twist at each increment.
  4. Release the torque and check for permanent set.

Precautions

  • Apply torque slowly and avoid impact.
  • Check that the shaft is truly circular and free of scale.
  • Read the protractor from the same eye level each time.
  • Do not exceed the elastic torque range.
Lab 03

Strut buckling

ObjectiveObserve the effect of end conditions and slenderness on the buckling load of a strut.

Method

  1. Measure the strut length and cross-section.
  2. Set the required end conditions and align the strut.
  3. Increase the axial load gradually while watching lateral deflection.
  4. Record the load at which deflection grows rapidly.

Precautions

  • Ensure the load is axial, not eccentric.
  • Do not lean on the frame during loading.
  • Stop before large deflections damage the strut.
  • Check the knife edges are seated correctly.

Lab 04

Tensile test of steel

ObjectiveObserve the stress–strain behaviour of mild and high-strength steel, identify yield, ultimate strength and fracture, and compare ductility.

Method

  1. Measure the specimen diameter at several positions and take the average.
  2. Mark the gauge length and mount the extensometer.
  3. Start the plotter and load steadily until fracture.
  4. Record the failure location and the appearance of the fracture surface.

Precautions

  • Check that the specimen is centred in the grips to avoid bending.
  • Do not touch the extensometer while the load is increasing.
  • Stop immediately if the specimen slips in the grips.
  • Keep clear of the machine during fracture.
Lab 05

Concrete cube compression and slump

ObjectivePrepare and test concrete cubes, and relate workability to the mix proportions.

Method

  1. Carry out the slump test on fresh concrete.
  2. Fill cube moulds in layers and compact each layer.
  3. Cure the cubes under the specified conditions.
  4. Test the cubes in compression and inspect the failure mode.

Precautions

  • Wear gloves and eye protection; cement is alkaline.
  • Compact uniformly to avoid honeycombing.
  • Label every cube and keep the curing record.
  • Centre the cube in the testing machine.

Lab 06

Orifice and Venturi flow

ObjectiveMeasure discharge through an orifice and a Venturi meter and compare with the theoretical flow.

Method

  1. Start the pump and establish steady flow.
  2. Purge air from the manometer tappings.
  3. Record the pressure heads and the collected volume over time.
  4. Change the flow rate and repeat.

Precautions

  • Remove air bubbles from manometer lines.
  • Allow flow to steady before timing.
  • Read the meniscus at eye level.
  • Do not run the pump dry.

Lab 07

Soil classification and compaction

ObjectiveClassify a soil from its grading and consistency limits, and determine its compaction characteristics.

Method

  1. Dry and quarter the sample.
  2. Carry out sieve and hydrometer analyses.
  3. Determine the liquid and plastic limits.
  4. Compact samples at increasing water contents and plot the dry density curve.

Precautions

  • Oven-dry samples at the specified temperature only.
  • Clean sieves and do not overload them.
  • Use distilled water for the hydrometer test.
  • Seal samples to prevent moisture change.
Lab 08

Permeability: constant and falling head

ObjectiveMeasure the coefficient of permeability of a soil using the method appropriate to its drainage.

Method

  1. Prepare and saturate the soil specimen.
  2. Set up the constant-head or falling-head apparatus.
  3. Allow flow to stabilise before taking readings.
  4. Record head, volume or time, and temperature.

Precautions

  • Saturate the specimen fully; trapped air changes the result.
  • Check for leakage around the specimen.
  • Use de-aired water where available.
  • Record water temperature to correct viscosity.
Lab 09

Oedometer consolidation

ObjectiveObtain the compression and swelling characteristics of a saturated clay and estimate consolidation parameters.

Method

  1. Trim the specimen into the oedometer ring.
  2. Assemble the cell and apply the seating load.
  3. Increase the load in stages, allowing consolidation between stages.
  4. Unload in stages and record swelling.

Precautions

  • Keep the specimen saturated throughout.
  • Do not disturb the specimen during trimming.
  • Allow enough time for each stage to finish.
  • Record the time of each reading.
Lab 10

Direct shear and triaxial testing

ObjectiveDetermine the shear strength parameters of a soil under controlled drainage conditions.

Method

  1. Prepare the specimen at the required density.
  2. Set up the shear box or triaxial cell and apply the confining stress.
  3. Shear the specimen at a constant rate.
  4. Record shear load, displacement and pore pressure where applicable.

Precautions

  • Maintain the specified drainage condition.
  • Check the loading rate is slow enough for the test type.
  • Seat the specimen without gaps at the edges.
  • Zero the instruments before loading.

Lab 11

Levelling and traverse survey

ObjectiveCarry out a closed levelling loop and a traverse, and check the misclosure before computing coordinates.

Method

  1. Reconnoitre the site and mark the stations.
  2. Run the levelling loop using the rise-and-fall or height-of-collimation method.
  3. Observe the horizontal angles and distances around the traverse.
  4. Reduce the observations and check the misclosure.

Precautions

  • Set the tripod firmly and check the bubble before every reading.
  • Keep the staff vertical and on the marked point.
  • Balance backsight and foresight distances where possible.
  • Record everything in the field book immediately.

Lab 12

Lateral–torsional buckling of a steel beam

ObjectiveObserve lateral–torsional buckling of a slender steel beam and the effect of load height and restraint.

Method

  1. Measure the beam section and span.
  2. Set the loading and restraint arrangement.
  3. Apply load in increments and monitor lateral deflection.
  4. Record the buckled shape and the controlling failure mode.

Precautions

  • Check the beam is laterally unrestrained where intended.
  • Apply load through the specified point.
  • Stop loading when the beam becomes unstable.
  • Keep the test area clear during loading.

Lab 13

Pipe friction and minor losses

ObjectiveMeasure head loss along a pipe and across fittings, and relate it to flow rate.

Method

  1. Open the pipe circuit and set a steady flow.
  2. Record the pressure tapping readings across the test section.
  3. Collect a measured volume over time for the flow rate.
  4. Repeat for several flow rates.

Precautions

  • Purge air from the tappings and manometer.
  • Check for leaks at joints.
  • Allow the flow to stabilise before reading.
  • Use the correct manometer range for the pressure difference.
Lab 14

Open-channel flow in a flume

ObjectiveObserve uniform and gradually varied flow, and measure the water surface profile.

Method

  1. Set the flume slope and tailgate.
  2. Establish a steady discharge.
  3. Measure depth and velocity at several sections.
  4. Change the control and observe the new profile.

Precautions

  • Do not stand in or obstruct the flume during measurement.
  • Check the flume is level laterally.
  • Allow waves to settle before measuring.
  • Keep electrical equipment away from the water.

Lab 15

Marshall mix design and CBR

ObjectivePrepare asphalt specimens for Marshall testing and determine the CBR of a compacted soil or aggregate.

Method

  1. Prepare and compact the specimens at the specified temperature.
  2. Measure bulk density and void properties.
  3. Test stability and flow, or carry out the CBR penetration test.
  4. Record the specimen details and test conditions.

Precautions

  • Control the mixing and compaction temperature.
  • Wear heat-resistant gloves when handling asphalt.
  • Keep the CBR surcharge and seating correct.
  • Do not reuse specimens for a second test.
