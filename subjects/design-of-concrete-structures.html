Reinforced concrete design turns a required strength into steel areas, bar sizes and cover. It links limit-state loading to bending, shear, slab and column checks, then closes with detailing and serviceability.

01Limit state design in concrete

Concrete resists compression well and tension poorly, so reinforcement is placed where a member would otherwise crack. Limit state design replaces one global safety factor with partial factors applied separately to loads and materials.

  • At the ultimate limit state (ULS) the member must not collapse.
  • At the serviceability limit state (SLS) deflection and cracking must stay acceptable in normal use.
  • The characteristic concrete strength f_cu is the 28-day cube strength; f_y is the characteristic yield strength of the reinforcement.

Never mix the two states. A section can pass ULS and still sag or crack unacceptably, and an SLS check says nothing about collapse.

Design load
\[w_u = 1.4G_k + 1.6Q_k\]

02Bending of a singly reinforced section

A rectangular section in bending is designed from a stress block in the concrete and a tensile force in the steel. The K value measures how heavily the concrete is stressed:

K = M / (f_cu b d²)

where b is the section width and d the effective depth, measured from the compression face to the centroid of the tension steel.

  • Below the limiting K′ = 0.156, a section can be singly reinforced.
  • The lever arm is z = d [0.5 + √(0.25 − K/0.9)], capped at 0.95d.
  • The steel area follows from A_s = M / (0.87 f_y z).
K value
\[K = \frac{M}{f_{cu} b d^2}\]
M in N mm, f_cu in N/mm², b and d in mm
Lever arm
\[z = d\left[0.5 + \sqrt{0.25 - \frac{K}{0.9}}\right] \le 0.95d\]
Required tension steel
\[A_s = \frac{M}{0.87 f_y z}\]

03Moment coefficients for continuous beams

For a continuous beam under uniform loading, a full elastic analysis can be replaced by tabulated moment coefficients when three conditions hold:

  • the spans differ by no more than about 15% of the longest,
  • the loading is essentially uniform,
  • the live load does not exceed the dead load.

The design moment is then M = β F L, where F = w L is the total design load on the span and β is the coefficient for the position. Typical values are 0.04F at an outer support, 0.075F near midspan of an end span, 0.086F at the first interior support, and 0.063F at midspan of interior spans and at interior supports. The first interior support moment is the largest, which is why top steel is heaviest there; the associated shear forces follow from the same table or from the free body of each span.

The coefficients assume monolithic construction and fairly uniform stiffness along the beam. Heavily haunched or irregular frames need an elastic analysis instead.

Design moment from coefficients
\[M = \beta F L\]
F = w L is the total design load on the span; β depends on the position

04Flanged beams and compression steel

Beams cast with a slab act as T- or L-sections, because a width of slab joins the compression zone. The effective flange width is limited to a fraction of the span and of the beam spacing, so only slab close to the web is counted.

Check where the stress block sits: its depth is s = 2(d − z). If s does not exceed the flange thickness h_f, the section behaves as a rectangle of the full flange width and the usual K and z expressions apply. If the block reaches into the web, design as a true T-section.

When K exceeds K′, deepen the beam or add compression steel, sized from the excess moment and the depth between the steel layers.

05Effective flange width and the T-beam sequence

A T-beam counts only part of the slab as its flange. The overhang on each side of the web is limited to about 0.2 times the clear distance to the adjacent beam plus 0.1 times the effective span, and for an edge (L) beam the span term is reduced to about 85% of that value. Worked from a beam grid with 4 m spacing and 5 m spans, the effective flange width came to 680 mm, giving 930 mm in total once the 250 mm web was added — far less than the full spacing.

The design sequence is then fixed:

  • compute K with the flange width,
  • find z,
  • check the stress block depth s = 2(d − z).

If s stays within the flange thickness h_f, the section is designed as a rectangle of width b_f; if s dips into the web, the compression in the web must be treated separately. Keep the flange-to-web width ratio below about 4, and check the minimum steel against the web area rather than the whole flange.

Effective flange width, interior T-beam
\[b_{\mathrm{eff}} = b_w + 0.2\,b_1 + 0.1\,\ell\]
b₁ is the clear distance between webs and ℓ the effective span; for an edge beam reduce the span term to about 0.85
Stress block depth check
\[s = 2(d - z)\]
if s ≤ h_f the section behaves as a rectangle of width b_f

06Shear and links

Shear is carried partly by the concrete and partly by links (stirrups). The applied stress is v = V/(b_v d), checked at a section d from the support face. The concrete contribution v_c comes from a code table and depends on the longitudinal steel ratio and the concrete grade.

The excess goes into links: A_sv/s_v ≥ (v − v_c) b_v / (0.87 f_yv). Spacing tightens near supports, where shear peaks, and the stress must stay below a crushing limit of roughly 0.8√f_cu.

Links also confine concrete, restrain main bars and hold the cage together, so codes require a minimum link area even where calculation demands none.

Required link area
\[\frac{A_{sv}}{s_v} \ge \frac{(v - v_c)b_v}{0.87 f_{yv}}\]

07Slabs in one and two directions

A slab is one-way when supported on two opposite edges and two-way when the longer span is under about twice the shorter. A two-way slab bends in both directions, so each strip carries only part of the load.

Thickness is usually set by deflection, not bending. The basic span-to-effective-depth ratio is about 20 for a simply supported slab and 26 for a continuous one, modified for the steel provided. Continuous slabs may use tabulated moment coefficients instead of a frame analysis.

Add distribution steel across the main bars to control cracking, and check punching shear around columns, where a concentrated reaction can punch through a thin slab.

08Columns: axial load and slenderness

A short column under mainly axial load has nominal capacity

N = 0.4 f_cu A_c + 0.75 f_y A_sc,

with A_c the net concrete area and A_sc the steel area. The coefficients account for cube-to-member strength differences and strain compatibility.

Slenderness changes the picture: a slender column bends, and the extra moment from that deflection adds to the applied moment. Design checks slenderness about both axes, applies a minimum eccentricity and considers biaxial bending.

Detailing still applies: at least about 1% steel, a maximum of 4–6%, four bars minimum in a rectangle, and links spaced no more than twelve bar diameters or the least dimension.

Short column axial capacity
\[N = 0.4f_{cu}A_c + 0.75f_yA_{sc}\]

09Cover, durability and detailing

Nominal cover is the distance from the surface to the outside of the reinforcement. It keeps water and chlorides away from the steel and insulates the steel in a fire, so exposure and fire resistance set it.

Bars must also develop their force. Anchorage length grows with bar diameter and stress, at roughly l_b = f_y φ / (4 f_b) where f_b is the design bond stress. Laps are longer, since two bars share one length of concrete.

Keep bars far enough apart for aggregate to pass, and hook links around the main steel. Detailing is where the calculation becomes buildable: the steel has to fit, be fixed and be surrounded by compacted concrete.

10Moment redistribution and the detailing package

Because a well-reinforced concrete section is ductile, the elastic peak moments at supports can be redistributed — shaved down and rebalanced elsewhere — provided the section has the rotation capacity. Up to about 30% redistribution is allowed while the neutral axis stays shallow, at x/d ≤ 0.5, tapering linearly to zero at x/d = 0.6.

Redistribution buys economy at supports, but it demands ductility: lightly reinforced sections with plenty of shear capacity qualify; heavily reinforced ones do not.

Detailing then locks the analysis in:

  • Steel area must sit between the minimum of about 0.13% of b h and the maximum of about 4% of the gross concrete area.
  • Curtail bars in low-moment zones.
  • Keep spacing close enough to control cracking yet wide enough for aggregate to pass.
  • Anchor or bend every bar whose force must enter the concrete.

A bar that ends where it is still needed is a designed-in crack.

Redistribution and neutral axis limit
\[\frac{x}{d} \le 0.6 - 0.33\,\beta\]
β is the fraction of moment redistributed; β = 0.3 gives x/d ≤ 0.5
Minimum tension steel
\[A_{s,\min} \approx 0.0013\,b\,h\]
about 0.13% of the gross section; the maximum is about 4%

11Serviceability: deflection and cracking

Deflection is controlled by limiting the span-to-depth ratio or by calculation against limits such as span/250. Creep and shrinkage add to the short-term elastic deflection, so sustained loads need a long-term estimate.

Cracking is controlled by distributing steel near the tension face and limiting bar spacing. A calculated width has the form

w_k = 3 a_cr ε_m / [1 + 2(a_cr − c_min)/(h − x)],

where a_cr is the distance to the nearest bar, ε_m the mean strain, c_min the minimum cover and x the neutral axis depth.

Keep steel stress moderate and bars closely spaced, and the width usually stays near the 0.3 mm limit.

Crack width
\[w_k = \frac{3a_{cr}\varepsilon_m}{1 + 2\frac{a_{cr} - c_{min}}{h - x}}\]

Key terms

Characteristic strength
The strength below which no more than a small specified proportion of test results is expected to fall.
Effective depth
Distance from the compression face to the centroid of the tension reinforcement.
Lever arm
Distance between the concrete compression resultant and the steel tension force, capped at 0.95d.
K value
Moment divided by f_cu b d squared; it shows whether a section can be singly reinforced.
Nominal cover
Specified distance from the concrete surface to the outside of a bar, set by durability and fire.
Punching shear
Concentrated shear around a column or load that can push a plug of slab through the support.
Moment redistribution
Deliberately shifting some elastic support moment into the spans, allowed only where the section has the ductility to rotate.
Effective flange width
The limited width of slab counted as part of a T- or L-beam's compression flange, set by span and beam spacing.
Worked example

Design the tension steel for a rectangular beam

A simply supported beam 250 mm wide and 500 mm deep carries a design ultimate moment of 200 kNm. The effective depth is 450 mm, f_cu = 30 N/mm² and f_y = 500 N/mm². Determine whether the section can be singly reinforced and find the required area of tension steel. State a practical bar arrangement.

  1. K = M / (f_cu b d²) = (200 × 10⁶) / (30 × 250 × 450²) = 0.132.
  2. Compare with K′ = 0.156: K is smaller, so no compression steel is needed.
  3. z = d [0.5 + √(0.25 − K/0.9)] = 450 [0.5 + √(0.25 − 0.1463)] = 450 × 0.822 = 370 mm.
  4. Check the cap: 0.95d = 427 mm, so the calculated z of 370 mm is used.
  5. A_s = M / (0.87 f_y z) = (200 × 10⁶) / (0.87 × 500 × 370) = 1243 mm².
  6. Check minimum steel: about 0.13% of b × h = 0.0013 × 250 × 500 = 163 mm², so the bending requirement governs.
  7. Sanity check: provide 4 H20 bars, A_s = 1256 mm². 100A_s/(b d) = 1.12%, below the maximum of about 4%.
AnswerProvide 4 H20 bars (1256 mm²) in the tension zone; K = 0.132 is below K′, so the beam is adequately singly reinforced.

Exam method

  • Convert everything to newtons and millimetres before using K, then state the final answer in kNm, mm² or MPa.
  • Always compare K with K′; a beam that needs compression steel is a different calculation from a singly reinforced one.
  • Cap the lever arm at 0.95d and state the cap — examiners award the check.
  • Separate ULS and SLS in the answer: strength checks and deflection or crack checks are marked as different requirements.
  • Before using moment coefficients, state the three conditions — near-equal spans, uniform loading, live load not exceeding dead load — and check them against the question.
