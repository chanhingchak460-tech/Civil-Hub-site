How steel members and connections are checked at the ultimate and serviceability limit states: classification, bending, shear, buckling, welds and bolts. It is where a section table, a design code and a calculator meet.

01Two numbers and a factor of safety

Limit state design compares a factored load effect with a factored resistance and requires the resistance to be the larger: E_d ≤ R_d.

  • Load factors cover uncertainty of dead, imposed and wind loads; material factors cover yield strength and fabrication.
  • Ultimate limit states cover strength, stability and fire; serviceability limit states cover deflection, vibration and durability.
  • The aim is a small, known probability of failure during the design life, not zero risk.
  • Ductility and robustness matter: a structure that warns before it fails is safer.
Limit state check
\[E_d \le R_d\]
R_d is the design resistance

02Steel, strength and slenderness

Steel is specified by grade — S275, S355, S460 — where the number is the minimum yield strength in N/mm² for that thickness. The design strength used in calculations is p_y = f_y / γ_M.

  • Steel is stiff, strong and ductile; ductility allows redistribution of force and gives visible warning before fracture.
  • Residual stresses from rolling and welding do not reduce the squash load, but they do reduce stiffness and bring buckling forward.
  • Toughness matters in thick sections and at low temperature, so the impact sub-grade belongs in the specification.
Design strength
\[p_y = \frac{f_y}{\gamma_M}\]
γ_M is the material factor, close to 1.0

03Section classification

Local buckling depends on how slender each plate element is. The width-to-thickness ratio b/T is compared with limits written as multiples of ε, where ε = √(275/p_y) and 275 N/mm² is the reference yield strength.

  • Class 1 plastic: can form a plastic hinge and rotate.
  • Class 2 compact: reaches the plastic moment with little rotation.
  • Class 3 semi-compact: reaches yield at the extreme fibre only.
  • Class 4 slender: buckles locally before yield.
  • The whole section takes the worst class of its elements.
Slenderness factor
\[\varepsilon = \sqrt{\frac{275}{p_y}}\]
p_y in N/mm²; the limits for b/T are written as multiples of ε

04Tension members: gross, net and effective area

Tension is the cleanest steel check — no buckling, just area times strength — but the detailing is subtle. The gross area A_g is the full cross-section; the net area A_n removes bolt holes; and the effective area A_e reduces further for shear lag: when bolts connect only one leg of an angle, the outstand leg joins the load gradually through the corner rather than at the connection.

  • Single angle bolted through one leg: A_n = A_g − 0.3 a₂, where a₂ is the area of the unconnected leg.
  • Double angle sharing the connection: A_n = A_g − 0.15 a₂.
  • Effective area: A_e = K_e A_n, with K_e of about 1.1 for S355 steel.
  • A tension test on 65 × 65 angles showed strain falling steeply across the outstand leg — shear lag made visible.
Net area, single angle bolted through one leg
\[A_n = A_g - 0.3\,a_2\]
a₂ is the area of the unconnected (outstand) leg
Net area, double angle
\[A_n = A_g - 0.15\,a_2\]
the smaller deduction recognises the better load transfer in a back-to-back pair
Effective area
\[A_e = K_e A_n\]
K_e is about 1.1 for S355; capacity follows from p_y A_e

05Bending and shear

The bending resistance of a Class 1 or 2 section is the plastic moment M_c = p_y S, where S is the plastic section modulus. A Class 3 section uses the elastic modulus Z instead, and a Class 4 section uses the effective modulus.

  • Shear is carried mainly by the web: V_c = 0.6 p_y A_v, where A_v is the shear area.
  • When the shear force exceeds about 60% of the shear resistance, the moment capacity must be reduced for the shear area lost.
  • Concentrated loads also require web bearing and web buckling checks, with stiffeners where the web is too thin.
  • Deflection is a separate serviceability check with unfactored loads.
Bending resistance, Class 1 or 2
\[M_c = p_y S\]
use Z for a Class 3 section
Shear resistance
\[V_c = 0.6\,p_y A_v\]
shear area, mainly the web

06Lateral-torsional buckling

An unrestrained beam bends about its strong axis, but the compression flange wants to move sideways. Once the flange displaces and the section twists, the beam fails by lateral-torsional buckling at a load well below the plastic moment.

The resistance is M_b = χ_LT M_c, where the reduction factor χ_LT falls as the slenderness λ_LT rises.

  • Slenderness depends on the effective length between restraints, the torsional and warping stiffness, and the load height.
  • Loads on the top flange are destabilising; loads on the bottom flange are stabilising.
  • The equivalent uniform moment factor allows for a non-uniform moment between restraints.
Buckling resistance moment
\[M_b = \chi_{LT} M_c\]
reduces the plastic moment for lateral-torsional buckling

07Compression members

A stocky column squashes when the stress reaches the design strength, so its capacity is close to p_y A_g. A slender column buckles first, and the capacity comes from the Perry-Robertson relationship between the yield strength, the Euler buckling stress and an imperfection parameter.

  • Real columns carry initial bow, residual stress and eccentricity, which is why the buckling curves are calibrated empirically.
  • The effective length L_E = K L reflects end restraint and frame sway; the slenderness is λ = L_E / r, using the radius of gyration about the weaker axis unless restraint prevents it.
  • A column in a sway frame behaves as if it were longer than its physical length.
Euler buckling stress
\[p_E = \frac{\pi^2 E}{\lambda^2}\]
λ is the slenderness L_E / r; p_E is compared with p_y
Compression resistance
\[P_c = p_c A_g\]
p_c is the compressive strength from the buckling curve, below p_y

08Connections: bolts and welds

Connections transfer the member forces and often govern the cost of a steel frame. A simple connection is designed for shear and rotation; a moment connection must also transfer bending.

  • Bolts are checked for shear, bearing, tear-out and block shear, and a bolt group may also carry torsion.
  • Bolt shear resistance is V_s = p_s A_s, using the shear area of the relevant failure plane.
  • Fillet welds carry force through their throat: for a leg size s the throat is 0.7s, and the resistance per unit length is 0.7 s p_w.
  • Weld symbols, minimum lengths and edge distances are detailing requirements; a connection that cannot be fabricated is not a design.
Bolt shear resistance
\[V_s = p_s A_s\]
A_s is the shear area on the failure plane; p_s is the shear strength
Fillet weld resistance per unit length
\[P_w = 0.7\,s\,p_w\]
s is the leg size; 0.7s is the throat thickness

09Serviceability, fire and durability

Deflection limits protect finishes and comfort; the limit is expressed as a fraction of the span and checked with unfactored loads. Long-span floors also need a vibration check, usually by keeping the natural frequency above a few hertz.

  • Fire resistance is provided by intumescent coating, boards or concrete filling; the required period depends on the building's use and height.
  • Corrosion protection follows the exposure: galvanising or painting in normal conditions, more protection near the sea or in industrial atmospheres.
  • Detailing that avoids water traps and allows inspection is cheaper than maintenance.
  • A steel structure is a system — bracing, connections and robustness ties included — not a collection of members.
Deflection, simply supported beam with UDL
\[\delta = \frac{5 w L^4}{384 E I}\]
serviceability loads, unfactored; E in N/mm², I in mm⁴

10The full beam check in one pass

A realistic beam design is a fixed sequence. Load the beam from its tributary area: a floor with 2.94 kPa of slab weight and 3.0 kPa imposed load combines as 1.4G + 1.6Q = 8.92 kPa at ULS. Slab aspect decides the load path — one-way when the long side exceeds about twice the short, otherwise two-way with triangular or trapezoidal reactions onto the beam.

  • Check section class (ε = √(275/p_y) against the b/T and d/t limits).
  • Check bending (M_c = p_y S).
  • Check shear (V_c = 0.6 p_y A_v).
  • For an unrestrained beam, check the buckling resistance M_b at the restrained length.
  • Close with serviceability: deflection under unfactored imposed load against a limit such as span/360, and a vibration check keeping the fundamental frequency above about 3–5 Hz for floors.

Keeping the sequence fixed prevents the classic omission — a beam that passes bending but whose lateral restraint was never verified.

Fundamental frequency, simply supported beam
\[f = \frac{\pi}{2 L^2}\sqrt{\frac{E I}{m}}\]
m is the mass per unit length including the floor; keep f above about 3–5 Hz

Key terms

Limit state design
Design that checks factored load effects against factored resistances for both ultimate and serviceability conditions.
Design strength
The yield strength divided by a material factor, used as the stress limit in member checks.
Section classification
The grouping of a cross-section as plastic, compact, semi-compact or slender according to the slenderness of its plate elements.
Plastic section modulus
The first moment of the section area about the equal-area axis, giving the plastic moment when multiplied by the design strength.
Lateral-torsional buckling
Failure of an unrestrained beam by sideways displacement and twisting of its compression flange.
Effective length
The buckling length of a member between points of lateral restraint, adjusted for end conditions and frame sway.
Shear lag
The uneven spread of axial force across a connected section, because outstand parts join the load only through the connection.
Warping constant
A section property measuring resistance to non-uniform twisting, which a beam mobilises as it twists during lateral-torsional buckling.
Worked example

Bending, shear and deflection of a floor beam

A simply supported beam of span 7.5 m carries an ultimate uniformly distributed load of 30 kN/m and a service uniformly distributed load of 20 kN/m. The section is Class 1 with plastic modulus S = 1500 cm³, shear area A_v = 3500 mm² and second moment of area I = 3.6 × 10⁸ mm⁴. Design strength p_y = 355 N/mm² and E = 205 kN/mm². Check bending, shear and deflection against a span/360 limit.

  1. Maximum bending moment: M = w L² / 8 = 30 × 7.5² / 8 = 210.9 kNm.
  2. Bending resistance: M_c = p_y S = 355 × 1 500 000 = 532.5 × 10⁶ Nmm = 532.5 kNm. Since 210.9 < 532.5 kNm, bending is adequate at about 40% utilisation.
  3. Maximum shear: V = w L / 2 = 30 × 7.5 / 2 = 112.5 kN. Shear resistance: V_c = 0.6 p_y A_v = 0.6 × 355 × 3500 = 745.5 kN. Since 112.5 < 745.5 kN, shear is adequate and no moment-shear reduction is needed.
  4. Deflection with service load: δ = 5 w L⁴ / (384 E I) = 5 × 20 × 7500⁴ / (384 × 205 000 × 3.6 × 10⁸) = 11.2 mm. The limit is L/360 = 20.8 mm, so deflection is also adequate.
  5. All three checks pass. Bending gives the highest utilisation, so a lighter section could be tried, but lateral-torsional buckling and the connection design would then need separate verification.
AnswerBending 211 kNm < 533 kNm, shear 113 kN < 746 kN and deflection 11.2 mm < 20.8 mm, so the beam is adequate for these checks. Bending is the controlling check.

Exam method

  • Classify the section before calculating any capacity; the class decides whether the plastic modulus, the elastic modulus or effective properties are used.
  • Keep S and Z straight: S is for plastic design, Z for elastic design, and mixing them is a common lost mark.
  • Lateral-torsional buckling often governs a real beam, so check the restraint spacing before declaring a section adequate.
  • Use unfactored loads for deflection and factored loads for strength; state which set you are using in each line.
  • In an LTB question, list the restraint positions and where the load hangs before using any formula; both change the effective length and the answer.
